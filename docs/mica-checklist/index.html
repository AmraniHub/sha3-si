MiCA website requirements: the checklist

What the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation and ESMA expect to find on a CASP's website, duty by duty. Updated 2026-11-07.

A disclosure counts as prominent when a visitor can reach it within two clicks of the homepage. This is the yardstick the Dutch AFM applied in its 2026 review of crypto-asset service providers.

1. Authorisation statement

MiCA Art. 66(2); ESMA statement ESMA35-1872330276-2329 · source

Information to clients must be fair, clear and not misleading, and marketing must be recognisable as marketing (Art. 66(2)). ESMA expects authorised firms to say they are authorised, and by whom, and not to let that status cover services outside MiCA.

2. Pricing, costs and fees policy

MiCA Art. 66(4) · source

Firms must make their policies on pricing, costs and fees public, in a prominent place on their website (Art. 66(4)). A sales page that promises "low fees" is not a policy: say how prices are formed (spreads, mark-ups), which fees apply to which service, and how changes are announced.

3. Climate and environmental impact

MiCA Art. 66(5); Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/422 · source

Firms must publish, in a prominent place on their website, the principal adverse climate and environmental impacts of the consensus mechanism behind each crypto-asset they serve (Art. 66(5)). Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/422 sets the indicators, such as energy consumption, renewable share and emissions, and the reporting period. A corporate environmental policy does not replace per-asset indicators.

4. Complaints procedure

MiCA Art. 71(1)-(3); Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/294 · source

Firms must have and publish a complaints-handling procedure, let clients complain free of charge, tell them they can complain, and make a complaint template available (Art. 71(1)-(3)). Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/294 adds the detail on the procedure and the template.

5. Links to white papers

MiCA Art. 66(3) · source

Firms that run a trading platform, exchange crypto-assets for funds or other crypto-assets, give advice or manage portfolios must give clients hyperlinks to the white papers of the crypto-assets they provide those services for (Art. 66(3)).

6. Risk warning

MiCA Art. 66(3) · source

Firms must warn clients of the risks of crypto-asset transactions (Art. 66(3)). A warning hidden in the footer while the page above promotes a yield is the pattern supervisors keep pointing to.

7. Regulated and unregulated services told apart

ESMA statement ESMA35-1872330276-2329 · source

Some staking, lending, earn and DeFi products fall outside MiCA. ESMA has warned firms not to present them under their MiCA authorisation, and to label them clearly as outside it (ESMA35-1872330276-2329).

8. Register entry matches the website

ESMA interim MiCA register · source

ESMA's interim register lists each authorised firm with its website. When that address does not open the firm's own site, or the site never names the registered legal entity, clients and supervisors cannot connect the two.
