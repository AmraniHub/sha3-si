    Frequently asked

    Is this legal advice?

    No. sha3.si records what your website showed, when, and from where, and explains which MiCA duty each finding relates to. Whether something is a breach is for your compliance team, your counsel and your supervisor to decide.

    Who runs sha3.si?

    sha3.si is a service of Servolia LLC, a Wyoming (USA) company. Scans run on servers in Frankfurt am Main, Germany. We are not a law firm, and we never hold, move or exchange crypto-assets.

    What does the free scan read?

    Your homepage, robots.txt and sitemap, up to six legal or disclosure pages linked from the homepage, the disclosure pages it finds, and one asset page when it looks for white papers: about twenty requests, made like a desktop browser. It never submits a form and never clicks a consent banner.

    Why "two clicks"?

    MiCA asks for fee and climate disclosures "in a prominent place" on the website. The Dutch AFM, reviewing crypto firms in 2026, looked for cost information within one or two clicks of the homepage. We use the same yardstick.

    What does Claude do?

    Claude, Anthropic's AI model, reads the public text of the pages the scanner found and judges what they say against each duty: is the fee page a real policy, does the climate page give indicators per crypto-asset, does the complaints page offer a template. It quotes your page exactly and says "unclear" when the text does not allow a judgment. No personal data is sent to it.

    Can I check the evidence without trusting you?

    Yes. Every page we read has a SHA3-256 fingerprint of the exact bytes received, every report is sealed with its own fingerprint, and each day the fingerprints are folded into a Merkle tree whose root is timestamped in Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps. The verification page recomputes the Merkle proof in your browser, and the open-source ots client checks the Bitcoin proof.

    My site is built with JavaScript. Does the scan still work?

    The free scan reads the HTML your server sends, so menus drawn later by JavaScript can be missed; the report says so when it happens. The paid audit adds a full browser pass.

    What do you store?

    The public pages we fetched, the reports, and their fingerprints. Your email and name only if you send them to us. Your IP address is never stored in clear: a salted hash is kept for an hour to stop abuse. No cookies, no tracking.
